About

We invest here because we live here

We are not a fund and we are not an agency. We put our own capital into buildings in Interior Alaska and on the North Slope, bring them back into good order, and stay to run them. Our office is in one of them.

What we do

Fairbanks Commercial Properties is a privately held real estate operating company working across residential, commercial and mixed use property in Interior Alaska. We look after roughly 400,000 square feet: 201 homes, office suites and warehouses let to local businesses, two aircraft hangars, and 234 bedrooms of crew accommodation on the North Slope.

Part of the Northward Building is income qualified, which means households the open market prices out of downtown can still live there. We think that matters, and we have no plans to convert those units.

The mix across the portfolio is deliberate. Apartments in Fairbanks follow one set of economics, slope industrial and crew housing follow another, and the two rarely go soft at the same time. That stability is what lets us keep investing when other owners pull back.

How we are set up

Acquisition, renovation, leasing and day to day management all sit with the same team, working out of our own building at 300 Barnette Street. Nothing is handed to an outside manager once the deal closes, which is why a tenant with a problem reaches someone who can authorize the fix on the same call.

The team's background runs through finance, engineering, construction and field operations, built up both here and overseas. We hire locally, we spend with Fairbanks trades and suppliers wherever we can, and the people making decisions about these buildings drive past them every day.

Our mission

Building so people stay

We want Interior Alaska to be a place people choose to stay in. That means homes families can settle into, workplaces that hold up through a Fairbanks winter, and buildings this town will still be glad of in fifty years.

Our philosophy

Value gets built, not bought

Nothing of value arrives at closing. It gets put in afterwards, by people rather than by the building itself. We decide quickly, we move quickly, and we give our attention to the unglamorous parts of the job, because that is where a building is won or lost.
